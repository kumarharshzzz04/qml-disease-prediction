Methodological Integrity & Transparency

On the official fixed Cleveland benchmark split, the Hybrid Quantum Committee ties the tuned classical SVM at 85.25%. Rather than claiming unwarranted quantum supremacy, Quemeds demonstrates that combining quantum projective kernel maps and variational representations with classical linear bounds produces an ensemble that matches the best classical models on tabular clinical data while providing novel quantum representations and explainability.

Scientific Methodology

Architectural specification of the hybrid quantum-classical pipeline, feature encoding, quantum circuits, and out-of-fold stacking meta-learner.

Problem & Clinical Motivation

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading cause of global mortality. Traditional statistical risk calculators (Framingham, ASCVD) suffer from calibration drift across diverse patient demographics. Quemeds investigates whether hybrid quantum-classical machine learning can extract non-linear geometric patterns in clinical biomarker spaces.

5-Stage Data Preprocessing
1. Imputation: Median replacement fit strictly on training folds.
2. StandardScaler: Zero-mean unit-variance normalization.
3. Supervised ANOVA F-test: Selects the top-4 features (Thalassemia, Max Heart Rate, Major Vessels, Exercise Angina).
4. Angle Scaling: Features are mapped to [0, π] for quantum rotation gates.
5. Target Binarization: Cleveland stages 0 → healthy, 1–4 → disease present.
4-Qubit Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC)
• Register: 4 qubits simulated via PennyLane (`default.qubit`).
• Data Re-uploading: 4 alternating layers of parameter-scaled RY(θ) rotations and trainable unitary rotations.
• Entanglement Topology: Ring topology of controlled-NOT (CNOT) gates between adjacent qubits (qi → qi+1, q3 → q0).
• Measurement: Expectation values of multi-qubit Pauli-Z operators ⟨σz(i)⟩.
Leakage-Free Stacking Meta-Learner

To eliminate data leakage, member predictions are generated via 5-fold cross-validated out-of-fold (OOF) inference. A calibrated Logistic Regression meta-learner computes optimal weights across the quantum and classical members, tuning the decision threshold strictly on training folds.

Benchmark Context: On the official fixed Cleveland benchmark split, the Hybrid Quantum Committee ties the tuned classical SVM at 85.25% (52/61 test patients). With n = 61, binomial test noise is approximately ±5 percentage points (each single patient corresponds to 1.64 pp, and independent cross-split re-derivations yield a mean of ~80% ± 2.5%). Rather than asserting unwarranted quantum supremacy, Quemeds demonstrates that integrating 3 real quantum circuits (Quantum Kernel SVM, Variational Quantum Classifier, and Bagged VQC Ensemble) within a leakage-free out-of-fold stacked committee successfully matches top classical baselines on tabular medical records while providing orthogonal quantum representations and full explainability.

Limitations & Research Boundaries
• Tabular QML Boundaries: Classical decision trees and kernel methods are mature and highly efficient on tabular clinical features. Quantum circuits provide orthogonal nonlinear representation spaces, but do not exhibit asymptotic speedups on small tabular records.
• Qubit Scale: Statevector simulation is bounded to 4 qubits via PennyLane (`default.qubit`) due to O(2n) classical memory scaling. Real quantum hardware execution remains subject to gate noise and shot variance.
• Sample Size & Noise: The primary Cleveland dataset contains 303 patients. Binomial test noise on n = 61 underscores that small differences (±1–2 patients) are statistical noise, reinforcing the necessity of honest, leakage-free benchmark reporting.
• Clinical Scope: Quemeds is an academic research prototype for hybrid quantum-classical algorithms and is strictly not intended for primary clinical diagnosis without prospective medical trial validation.
